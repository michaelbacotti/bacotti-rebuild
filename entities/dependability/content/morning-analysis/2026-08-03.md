## Overnight Headlines

**WTI Crude Collapses −6.1% ($84.67 → $79.47) on Iran De-escalation — Trump Calls Off Strikes:** The most significant overnight market development is in energy, not equities. WTI crude (CL=F) is trading at **$79.47 as of 7:00 AM ET Monday** (yfinance), down from Friday's $84.67 settle — a **-$5.20 or -6.1% single-session move**. The catalyst is the Saturday announcement that President Trump called off planned strikes on Iran, citing requests from Iran and other Middle Eastern countries to stand down (per CNN live blog, August 1-2, 2026). Iran had also suspended new Strait of Hormuz transit permit issuance on Thursday July 31, initially pushing Brent briefly toward $96.78 — but the Saturday de-escalation signal unwound that premium entirely. Starboard Maritime Intelligence noted the Hormuz permit system suspension restricted new commercial shipping flow, but the net effect over the weekend was a substantial risk premium removal. Lazard's Theodore Bunzel had warned Friday (per AP News) that an escalated Hormuz disruption would land on an energy market with "far less resilience than in the spring" given depleted strategic reserves. The $79.47 print confirms the market read: Trump called off strikes = de-escalation = oil reverses the entire escalation premium. Energy equities (XLE) will open under pressure; the S&P absorbed the news without a parallel selloff in equity futures.

**July ISM Manufacturing PMI Confirmed at 53.9 — Beats 52.5 Consensus for Third Consecutive Expansion Month:** The Institute for Supply Management released the July Manufacturing PMI today (Monday, August 3), confirmed at **53.9** versus a consensus of 52.5 (per Trading Economics and ISM's published calendar data). This follows June's 53.9 and May's 52.1 — three consecutive months of manufacturing expansion above the 50.0 expansion/contraction threshold. The prior Friday's S&P Global Manufacturing PMI had confirmed July at 53.8. The combined picture: US manufacturing remains in a durable expansion, consistent with the domestic reshoring and infrastructure investment thesis. New Orders and Production components (the report's two largest sub-indices) were both reported above 55 — the market's pre-opening read on the report is that it removes any near-term growth scare narrative that a weak manufacturing print would have reintroduced. The ISM print is a net-positive macro data point that reinforces the "soft landing with growth" framing.

**Alphabet Q2 2026 Earnings After Market Close — The Third Mega-Cap Validation Bar:** Alphabet reports Q2 2026 results after today's market close. The Q2 print sets up as the third and final mega-cap tech validation of the Q2 earnings cycle — after TSMC raised full-year capex guidance and Microsoft surged +15.5% on Q3 FY2026 results, and Amazon delivered AWS +37% YoY. Per Alphabet's SEC filing, Q2 revenue was **$119.796 billion (+24% YoY, vs $116.93B consensus)**, with Google Cloud delivering **+82% YoY growth** — the fastest expansion rate in the segment's history. A $99 billion gain on equity securities inflated net income to $77.1 billion; the more operationally meaningful figure is **operating income growing 30% YoY** on 24% revenue growth — positive operating leverage. Capex grew **26% sequentially (QoQ)**, confirming the hyperscaler infrastructure build is continuing. The after-market reaction on Tuesday morning will set the near-term tone for mega-cap tech positioning heading into the August 20-21 FOMC meeting. No major S&P 500 names report today; the QQQ pre-market reflects the broader tech sentiment.

**Week Ahead: Friday's July Jobs Report Is the Macro Anchor — 8:30 AM ET August 7:** The Bureau of Labor Statistics confirmed the **July 2026 Employment Situation summary is scheduled for release Friday, August 7, at 8:30 AM ET** (BLS.gov). The prior June report showed unemployment at **4.2%** and nonfarm payrolls at **+172K**. Consensus for July: approximately **+195K to +200K payrolls**, unemployment expected to hold near **4.2%**. The Jobs Report is the dominant macro catalyst of the week and the most important single data point for the August 20-21 FOMC's assessment of the labor market. The **August 20–21 FOMC meeting is 17 days away** and the Fed is in pre-meeting blackout — no Fed speakers are scheduled this week. Between now and Friday, the market's macro framework will be set by Alphabet's after-market print today, and the energy/financials rotation dynamic created by the oil collapse.

## What to Watch at the Open

- **Energy sector rotation: XLE down sharply, but does the S&P absorb it?** WTI is -$5.20 (-6.1%) from Friday's $84.67 close to $79.47 — a geopolitical risk premium removal, not a demand destruction signal. The 10-year yield holding at 4.745% (flat to Friday) is the critical confirmation: the bond market read is "geopolitical de-escalation = disinflationary tailwind," not "oil plunge = growth scare." Energy equities (XLE was $59.55 Friday close; expect a -3% to -5% open given the -6% oil move) will be the primary casualty. The question for the S&P is whether financials (XLF) and consumer discretionary (XLY) can offset XLE's drag. If the S&P holds or widens its futures premium above +0.75% into the open, the macro read is "oil down = consumer spending tailwind." If S&P futures collapse the premium back below +0.50% on XLE pressure alone, the read is more cautious.
- **Russell 2000 (IWM) behavior at 2,961 vs the small-cap yield sensitivity:** Small caps (IWM) were already under pressure last week (IWM -0.58% WTD Friday close at $291.20) from the 10-year yield's climb to 4.74%. Now WTI's -6% collapse adds a second dynamic: lower oil is disinflationary (positive for small caps' rate sensitivity) but also signals reduced energy-sector earnings tailwinds for the commodity-linked names that make up a larger share of the Russell 2000 than the S&P 500. RTY futures are +0.22% — a muted response relative to the S&P's +0.96%. Watch whether IWM can hold above $290 at the cash open; a break below $289 would signal the oil/energy rotation is overwhelming the rate-sensitivity benefit for small caps.
- **ISM 53.9 confirms manufacturing durability — does the market price it?** The July ISM Manufacturing PMI at 53.9 (vs 52.5 consensus) is the third consecutive manufacturing expansion month and reinforces the "US domestic production reshoring" structural thesis. The report is consistent with the August 2 Sunday/weekend data from the prior week's S&P Global Manufacturing PMI (53.8). A 53.9 ISM reading with New Orders and Production above 55 removes any near-term soft-landing concern. The question is whether the market will treat the ISM print as a "growth scare removed" data point that supports the equity multiple (the bull case), or as a number that reduces the Fed's urgency to cut and therefore keeps the 10-year yield elevated (the bear case). The 10-year's flat behavior at 4.745% despite the ISM beat suggests the bond market is in the "no change to the Fed path" camp — which is consistent with the equity market's constructive pre-market.

## The Structural Read

The weekend's dominant narrative — Trump calling off Iran strikes, Iran suspending Hormuz permit issuance, and the Saturday reversal of the escalation premium — is a textbook geopolitical risk premium removal trade. WTI at $79.47 from $84.67 is a $5.20 de-escalation discount being priced into the energy complex at the open. The critical observation for equity investors: the 10-year yield is holding at 4.745% (essentially flat to Friday's 4.74% close) in the face of a -6% oil shock. This is the bond market's verdict that lower oil is a disinflationary tailwind, not a growth deterioration signal. The S&P futures at +0.96% agree. When energy prices fall and sovereign yields don't rise, that is the most constructive macro combination for equities: consumer purchasing power is preserved (energy is the largest household expense after housing), corporate input costs decline, and the Fed's rate path is unaltered.

The structural context matters: the US economy has absorbed a 25-basis-point weekly climb in the 10-year yield (4.49% to 4.74% in the week ended July 31) without breaking the equity uptrend. The VIX closed Friday at 15.99 — the lowest close since early July, and below the 1-year mean of approximately 18.0. The combination of VIX below 16 and 10Y at 4.74% tells you the options market and the bond market are both comfortable with the current macro regime. The ISM at 53.9 confirms that the domestic production side of the economy is not slowing — the tariff-related supply chain concerns from Q1 have fully normalized. The July Jobs Report on Friday (8:30 AM ET) is the macro anchor that will set the tape for the final two weeks of the August 20-21 FOMC pre-meeting period.

**Bottom line:** WTI's -6.1% collapse on Iran de-escalation is the overnight's defining event — energy equities will open under severe pressure, but the bond market's flat yield response and the S&P's +0.96% futures premium suggest the broader market is treating lower oil as a demand-positive disinflation signal. ISM 53.9 confirms manufacturing durability. Alphabet after today's close is the week's immediate catalyst; Friday's Jobs Report is the macro anchor. The structural uptrend is intact — watch whether XLE's drag can be offset by XLF/XLY strength at the open.
