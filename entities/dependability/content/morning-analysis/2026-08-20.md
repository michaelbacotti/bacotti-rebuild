# Morning Market Analysis — August 20, 2026

## Overnight Headlines

**Gold extends its post-FOMC-minutes bid above $4,540 — pressing against the late-spring high (3-month peak at $4,591.80 on May 29).** Gold futures (GC=F) traded as high as $4,544.30 in the pre-market session, up +1.21% from yesterday's $4,489.40 futures close. The move extends the post-FOMC-minutes "committee-staff-path-still-patient" thesis: a more patient Fed path implies a structurally weaker dollar, real rates lower for longer, and continued central-bank diversification — all three are tailwinds for gold. The macro tape reinforced the move: DXY weakened -0.85% yesterday and continues weaker in the overnight session (98.69 vs 98.83 Wednesday close), while 10-year yields remain anchored around 4.65%. **WTI crude breaks higher to $86.72 (+1.03% pre-market), extending a five-day higher-low sequence as the dollar weakens and risk-premium accumulates.** Yesterday's session saw crude close flat (USO +0.19% to $130.91, WTI $85.83) as the FOMC minutes released the "patient staff path" view; overnight the move has resumed on a softer dollar and continued geopolitical risk-premium accumulation in the Middle East complex. The 5-day WTI trajectory ($84.50 → $84.94 → $85.83 → $86.73) reads as a clean higher-low sequence, though the front-month remains ~12% below the late-May peak (USO $150.54 on May 20). The structural implication: the cross-asset signal (gold ↑, crude ↑, dollar ↓, yields flat) is the canonical "renewed-cut-hopes + commodity-led" regime.

**Philadelphia Fed Manufacturing Index consensus looks for a sharp drop to 25 in August, from July's 41.4 — a 16-point deceleration.** The consensus 25 vs prior 41.4 is the widest gap in the recent sample; the prices-paid component has been steadily cooling (53.9 prior) and prices-received at 27.4 prior. The August release is the first hard-data checkpoint for manufacturing in Q3 and arrives the same morning as jobless claims (forecast 208.5K vs prior 209K — essentially flat) and continuing claims (1,750K vs 1,777K — declining). A Philly Fed print at or below consensus would extend the disinflation-not-stagflation tape; a surprise to the upside (above 30) could give the cyclical cohort a bid back.
