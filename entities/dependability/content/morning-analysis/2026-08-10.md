## Overnight Headlines

**Asia Firmer Across the Board — Nikkei +2.08%, Hang Seng +1.05% as the Friday Record Close Echoes Globally.** Japan's Nikkei 225 closed at 66,970.22 Monday local time, up +2.08% from Friday's 65,606.71 — the largest single-session move in two weeks. Hang Seng +1.05% to 25,937.49, KOSPI +0.65%, TWSE +1.59% to 44,928.76. The driver: Asian markets are repricing for a lower terminal rate path now that the Warsh Fed's hawkish-dissent posture (3 dissents at the July 29 FOMC) faces fresh pressure from Friday's -23K payroll print. Japanese exporters caught the bid as USD weakness expectations extend; Hang Seng caught the bid as China-sensitive sectors priced the lower-rate regime. European markets opened mixed — DAX +0.40% to 26,423.79, FTSE -0.25% to 10,874.02, CAC +0.05% — consistent with Europe still digesting its own earnings season.

**Oil Bid Overnight — WTI +1.7% to $79.49 on Iran/Hormuz Uncertainty.** WTI crude futures (CL=F Sep 26 per yfinance) climbed to $79.49 Monday morning, up $1.31 (+1.7%) from Friday's $78.18 settle — a notable overnight extension that pulls WTI back above $78 and erases a portion of the late-July Iran-de-escalation-driven decline. USO is +2.25% to $121.54 per public.com. The driver: weekend reporting from Bloomberg, Reuters, and CNN confirmed Iran and Oman are "very close" to a deal on a temporary maritime transit route in the Strait of Hormuz, but Iran will demand US concessions before reopening — per Fortune's August 7 framing of "the emerging deal reportedly recognizes Iran's control." **The structural read: a confirmed Hormuz reopening would push Brent back below $75 and re-expand the equity multiple; a deal breakdown would force Brent above $90 and reintroduce the inflation-fear narrative ahead of Wednesday's CPI.**

**Gold Adds to Its Friday Surge — GC +1.3% Overnight, GLD +2.06%.** Gold futures (GC=F Dec 26 per yfinance) added +1.31% to $4,397.50 from Friday's $4,340.70 settle, and the GLD ETF is +2.06% to $397.70 per public.com. The overnight extension builds on Friday's +7.20% WTD GLD gain (the second-largest weekly gain of 2026) and reflects the Fed-cut-hope + safe-haven + USD-suppression framework that drove Friday's equity record close. The dollar index (DXY) is +0.16% to 99.696 — a modest uptick from Friday's 99.539 — consistent with the broader Goldilocks setup. The 10-year Treasury yield held flat at 4.66% Monday — the bond market is not reacting to the oil/gold moves with alarm, the cleanest signal that the disinflation regime remains intact into Wednesday's CPI.

## The Structural Read

The post-Friday-record-close tape is holding its gains. S&P 500 futures are +0.37% above Friday's cash close of 7,757.64 (a fresh all-time closing record), Nasdaq futures +0.31% above Friday, and the bond market is essentially flat at 4.66% on the 10Y with the VIX only modestly higher at 15.45. **The structural framing Monday: Friday's payrolls-shock-record-close combination validated the soft-landing-with-Fed-cut-hope thesis without breaking the technical setup, and Monday's quiet open is the market consolidating that combination into a sustainable bid ahead of Wednesday's CPI.** The AI capex durability thesis — five mega-cap Q2 bars (TSMC, MSFT, AMZN, Palantir, AMD) plus the implied sixth bar (NVDA Q2 late August) — remains the structural call, and the cumulative read across the week (SPX +2.07% WTD, Nasdaq +4.86% WTD) absorbed the Warsh-dissent repricing, the Hormuz progress, the post-FOMC vol spike, and the -23K Jobs Report without breaking the uptrend.

The oil +1.7% overnight move is the freshest variable. **Iran's "very close" but unconfirmed Hormuz reopening has pulled WTI back into the $79 area, and the corresponding XLE pre-market weakness (-1.14% per public.com) suggests the equity market is pricing the deal-completion scenario rather than the deal-breakdown scenario.** A confirmed Hormuz reopening would push WTI back into the $74-76 range and reinforce the disinflation trade into Wednesday's CPI; a deal breakdown would force a WTI retest of $85-90 and reintroduce the inflation-fear narrative. **The WTI at $79.49 sits roughly midway between these two scenarios, and the equity market is positioned for the deal-completion outcome.**

The CPI print Wednesday is the dominant macro event of the week, and consensus is positioned for the disinflation trend to hold (core +0.2% MoM; headline flat at 0.0% MoM; +3.4% YoY unchanged). **A clean in-line print confirms the soft-landing-with-Fed-cut-hope framework and supports a continued bid into the August 20-21 FOMC first Warsh SEP/dot plot; a hot 0.3%+ core print re-validates the Warsh-dissent framework and forces a multiple-compression test of the 7,650-7,700 area.** The structural bullish case — long-term US equity holders positioned for the AI capex durability, US manufacturing reshoring, disinflation trend, and Fed-cut window opening — has not been broken by Friday's record close or Monday's pre-market. It is being tested, not broken.

**Bottom line:** Monday is a quiet open after Friday's record close — a market digesting and repositioning ahead of Wednesday's CPI print and the week's Hormuz-deal-framework binary. Asia firmer (Nikkei +2.08%, Hang Seng +1.05%), Europe mixed (DAX +0.40%, FTSE -0.25%), US futures modestly higher (ES +0.08%, NQ +0.31%), oil +1.7% on Iran-deal uncertainty, gold +1.3% extending the Fed-cut-hope trade. The structural tape remains constructive: 7,757.64 holds as the record close, 4.66% is the 10Y pivot, 15.45 is the VIX pivot, and $79 is the WTI pivot. What would change the read: a sub-consensus CPI print Wednesday re-opening the 50bp-cut window (constructive extension); a hot CPI print forcing the 10Y toward 4.80% and a SPX pullback to 7,650 (multiple-compression test, not structural break); a confirmed Hormuz deal push oil below $76 and extend the equity multiple toward 7,800+ (cleanest catalyst); a Hormuz breakdown force oil back toward $85 and reintroduce the inflation-fear narrative (most-negative near-term scenario).
