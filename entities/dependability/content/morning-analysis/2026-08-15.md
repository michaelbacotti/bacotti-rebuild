This weekend read covers the week that was, the institutional positioning snapshot embedded in Friday's COT report, Asia's Friday response, and the calendar for the week ahead, with Jackson Hole as the structural pivot.

## Macro of the Week: The Disinflation Pipeline Confirmed

The week's two CPI-linked prints delivered what the market most wanted to hear: **inflation is decelerating on a monthly basis**.

**July CPI (August 13, released Wednesday):** Headline +0.1% MoM, core +0.1% MoM. Both below consensus (+0.2%/+0.2%). Annual headline CPI is now running at its slowest pace since late 2024. The market absorbed this cleanly — the 10Y drifted from 4.64% (pre-CPI) to 4.64% post-CPI, suggesting no further inflation premium being priced.

**July PPI (August 12, released Tuesday):** Headline 0.0% MoM (vs +0.2% consensus; June was -0.1%). Annual headline PPI at 4.7% — the slowest since late 2024. Core PPI did accelerate to +0.4% MoM on a 6.5% surge in portfolio management fees (an equity-correlated, volatile category), but the market treated the headline as dominant. The goods-deflation leg is intact.

**July Retail Sales (August 14, released Friday morning):** Headline -0.6% MoM versus +0.3% consensus. The widest miss since early 2023. Control group -0.5% MoM. This single print flipped the week's narrative: from "disinflation confirmed — Fed cuts incoming" to "disinflation confirmed — but is demand collapsing too?" The 10Y repriced from 4.64% to 4.704% (+6.4 bp) intraday. The CME FedWatch September 25 bp cut probability, already elevated at ~65% post-CPI, moved to approximately **70%** following the Retail Sales miss. A 50 bp cut is now being discussed in some corners.

**The structural read:** The CPI/PPI pair has done the work. Inflation is decelerating. The question the market is now wrestling with is not "will the Fed cut?" but "is the economy slowing too fast?" The August 19 FOMC Minutes (see below) will be the next structured read on how the committee itself is answering that question.

## Asia Overnight: China's Stimulus Attempt and Japan's Yen Pivot

Asia markets traded Friday and into the weekend with several notable data points.

**China's Friday data calendar was heavy.** Industrial production, retail sales, and fixed asset investment all missed consensus, reinforcing the demand-weakness narrative that has been the PBOC's primary concern for the past year. The PBOC responded by injecting approximately $50 billion via a Medium-Term Lending Facility (MLF) operation — the largest single MLF injection in several months. The signal: Beijing is prepared to add liquidity to hold the growth line, but has not yet shifted to the broader fiscal expansion that markets have been awaiting.

**Japan continues its rebalancing trade.** The Nikkei closed Friday (Japan time) up 0.6%, with the yen strengthening against the dollar providing a tailwind to domestically-focused names. The USD/JPY pair has been drifting lower as the Bank of Japan signals continued gradual normalization — a structural headwind for the carry trade but a tailwind for Japanese domestic equities.

**Hong Kong and broader Asia had a solid session.** The Hang Seng closed up 1.1% Friday (Hong Kong time), led by financials and Chinese tech. KOSPI (South Korea) gained 0.4%, and the ASX (Australia) added 0.3%. The relative outperformance of Asian equities versus the US Friday close reflects two things: (1) the growth scare was a US-specific consumer story, not a global demand collapse, and (2) the DXY softness (+70% September cut probability) is a dollar-headwind tailwind for EM and Asia.

## Next Week's Calendar: August 18–22

**Tuesday August 18 — No major US data.** No major US economic releases are on the calendar; global markets are in a quiet pre-FOMC window. Light session into tomorrow's structural gate.

**Wednesday August 19 — The week's structural gate.** Two significant releases:

- **8:30 AM ET: July Building Permits and Housing Starts.** Consensus for permits: 1.43 million (vs 1.45 million prior). Consensus for starts: 1.31 million (vs 1.33 million prior). The housing market remains under pressure from elevated mortgage rates (the 10Y's 4.7% handles roughly 7.0–7.2% mortgage rates). Any downside surprise extends the rate-sensitivity narrative.
- **2:00 PM ET: FOMC Meeting Minutes from the July 28–29 Meeting.** This is the single most important document of the week. The July FOMC meeting was the one where the committee held rates but began signaling a September cut. The minutes will reveal the degree of dispersion within the committee on three questions: (1) Is September cut the base case or still debated? (2) How are SEP (Summary of Economic Projections) dot-plot revisions being framed internally? (3) What is the committee's read on the labor market — is it still "robust" or has the cooling narrative taken hold? Any hint of a 50 bp cut discussion would be market-moving.

**Thursday August 20 — Existing Home Sales.** 10:00 AM ET. Consensus: 5.39 million units (vs 5.37 million prior). The existing home sales market is constrained by the "lock-in effect" — homeowners with sub-4% mortgages are unwilling to sell and buy at 7%+ rates, creating a structural inventory shortage. The sales pace has been range-bound for six months. Any meaningful beat or miss will be scrutinized for consumer-health signals.

**Friday August 21 — Manufacturing and Flash PMIs.** Three releases:

- **8:30 AM ET: July Philadelphia Fed Manufacturing Index.** Prior: 13.6. Consensus: 9.5. The Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index has been one of the more volatile regional surveys. A decline toward consensus (9.5) would be consistent with the soft-landing narrative. A reacceleration would be a positive surprise.
- **8:30 AM ET: Weekly Jobless Claims.** The weekly initial claims data will be watched closely after the Retail Sales miss raised questions about labor market resilience. Any tick above 250K consistently would reinforce the "Fed needs to cut" argument.
- **9:45 AM ET: S&P 500 Flash PMIs (August).** The composite and services PMI readings for August. Consensus: 54.5 composite (vs 54.3 prior). The PMI data is the most forward-looking indicator available, capturing new orders, employment, and business sentiment.

**Saturday August 22 — Quiet weekend ahead of the Jackson Hole window.** Markets closed. The Kansas City Fed's annual Jackson Hole Economic Symposium runs **Thursday August 27 through Saturday August 29** this year (per the August 14 PM forecast coverage). Fed Chair Jerome Powell speaks the following Friday at 10:00 AM ET in the marquee central-banking event of the year — markets will be watching for any shift in the tone of the "last mile" inflation fight language and whether the September cut is explicitly pre-committed. A repeat of the July FOMC press conference tone ("we have made no decisions") would be slightly hawkish relative to current 70%-priced September cut expectations. Any more dovish framing would push September cut probability above 75%.

## Cross-Asset Context

**VIX at 14.25 (Friday close, -6.7% over the four trading days from Tuesday's open, -7.8% on the canonical Monday-to-Friday basis per the Aug 14 PM):** The VIX is in steep contango — the 9-day implied volatility (^VIX9D) at 10.61 implies the market expects calm near-term conditions. The VIX term structure slope is approximately -25.5% (10.61 minus 14.25, divided by 14.25), well into the "protection is cheap" regime. This is consistent with the record SPX close and the absence of a near-term binary risk event until August 19.

**DXY at 99.67 (-0.15% WTD, -0.5% from prior Friday):** The dollar is softer. A DXY below 100 is historically associated with risk-on environments and dollar-funding ease. The combination of DXY below 100 and VIX in steep contango is a historically risk-asset-friendly configuration. The 99.67 level was last seen in mid-2025 before the tariff escalation period.

**Bitcoin at approximately $64,000:** Bitcoin has been range-bound between $62,000 and $66,000 for three weeks. The lack of directional conviction in crypto reflects the broader market's uncertainty — not enough to trigger risk-off selling, not enough momentum to break higher. The correlation with the DXY has reasserted: DXY softer = crypto bid; DXY stronger = crypto under pressure.

**Gold at approximately $4,380:** Gold is near a structural decision point. The $4,363 area was mentioned last week as a key support/pivot level (it was Thursday's intraday low before the record SPX close). Friday's close at $4,380 is above that pivot. The +54.44% OI hedge fund long position is historically elevated and represents a crowding risk: if gold breaks below $4,300, the de-crowding move could be sharp. The PBOC's weekend MLF injection and the China growth-scare narrative are both headwinds for gold in the near term.

## Positioning Overlay: What the COT Means for Next Week

The August 11 COT report (released Friday 3:30 PM ET) is the last institutional-positioning snapshot before Jackson Hole. Its key signals:

**S&P E-MINI flipped net long (+0.53% OI) — bullish signal for the broad market.** The ~38,500-contract swing from -1.29% OI to +0.53% OI in one week is the largest weekly repositioning in this instrument since the CPI shock in late 2025. Hedge funds are not just covering shorts — they are adding new longs. This is consistent with the record SPX close Thursday and the post-CPI "soft landing confirmed" thesis.

**Nasdaq-100 short deepened to -14.19% OI — the AI infrastructure cohort faces institutional skepticism.** The additional ~7,900-contract short in one week is significant. Applied Materials' -7.7% reaction to a beat-and-raise print is the kind of event that reinforces the "AI infrastructure is priced to perfection" view on the short side. **The risk: if Jackson Hole delivers a dove pivot, the Nasdaq-100 short covers aggressively and triggers a sharp squeeze in QQQ.**

**The VIX in steep contango (-25.5% slope) is consistent with "protection is cheap" — and with potential squeeze dynamics.** When VIX is this low and positioning is this one-sided (broad market long, Nasdaq short), any surprise event (a hot PPI, a hawkish FOMC Minutes, a geopolitical shock) triggers a rapid VIX spike and short-covering in both the SPX and NQ positions. The market is historically prone to "short covering + vol spike" dynamics in low-VIX, one-sided positioning environments.

## What to Watch: Jackson Hole Is the Macro Pivot

The week of August 18 is structurally centered on **one document and one event** — the **August 19 FOMC Minutes** (Wednesday) and the **August 28 Powell Jackson Hole speech** (next Friday) — and **one question**: is the Fed about to commit to a September 25 bp cut, or is it keeping optionality open?

**The disinflation story is essentially confirmed.** CPI +0.1% MoM, PPI 0.0% MoM. The market has done its job pricing a September cut. What the FOMC Minutes and Jackson Hole will reveal is whether the committee's own thinking has moved as fast as the market has.

**The growth scare story is the new variable.** July Retail Sales at -0.6% MoM is not a recession signal in isolation, but it is the most significant "demand below expectations" print since early 2023. If the August 19 FOMC Minutes reveal that committee members are now explicitly discussing the risk of labor market softening accelerating, the September cut probability moves toward 85%+ and the 10Y reprices toward 4.55%.

**The positioning setup going into Jackson Hole is unusually clean:** SPX E-MINI net long (bullish base), Nasdaq short (AI-cohort tension), VIX cheap (potential squeeze), gold near structural pivot ($4,363), DXY soft (99.67). A single dovish signal from Powell could trigger a broad short-covering rally that lifts all risk assets.

**Bottom line:** The week of August 18 is the most consequential macro week since the July FOMC itself. The disinflation pipeline has been confirmed; the growth scare has not yet been confirmed as durable. Jackson Hole is the next structural inflection point.
