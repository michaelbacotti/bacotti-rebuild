# Morning Market Analysis — August 07, 2026

Morning Market Analysis — Friday, August 7, 2026

The pre-Jobs-Report tape is orderly. S&P 500 futures sit just above Thursday's 7,709.96 close with a modest +0.16% overnight lift in ES, while the Nasdaq (+0.44% in NQ futures) is outpacing as megacap tech digests the +4.10% XLK week-to-date surge. The 10-year yield has climbed 5 basis points to 4.67% since Thursday's close — the bond market is positioning for a hot or at least resilient July payrolls print at 8:30 AM ET. The July Employment Summary is the sole macro catalyst today; the Fed is in blackout ahead of the August 20–21 FOMC meeting. A clear verdict on the labor market's direction — soft landing intact or margin re-narrowing — arrives before the weekend.

## Overnight Headlines

**NFP Consensus at +70K: June's +57K Miss Raises the Bar for July — Friday 8:30 AM ET.** The BLS publishes the July Employment Summary at 8:30 AM ET with a consensus estimate of +70,000 non-farm payrolls (per the forex.tradingcharts.com economic calendar for August 7), compared to June's +57,000 — itself a significant miss versus the prior consensus of approximately +110K. The participation rate is expected to tick up to 62.0% from 61.5%, which would add labor supply and could hold the unemployment rate steady at 4.2% even if payroll growth is modest. Average hourly earnings are expected unchanged month-over-month at 0.0% per the calendar. With ADP's private-sector print at +44,000 for July (released Wednesday) having set a lower floor, the market is braced for a sub-100K print — the question is whether it is the "transient summer slowdown" variety (harmless) or the "labor market is genuinely decelerating" variety (recession signal). The ISM Services employment gauge rebounding to 51.0 in July (from 46.2 in June) provides some offset to the ADP miss.

**Energy Surges on Iran Hormuz Draft Plan — XLE +1.48% in Thursday Close, Oil Lift Extends Overnight.** Energy was Thursday's strongest sector (+1.45% per yfinance close; +1.48% in yfinance Friday morning data vs Thursday's $77.29 close), driven by Iranian state media publishing a draft plan with restrictive conditions for ship traffic in the Strait of Hormuz — partially reopening the waterway but on terms that preserve oil-supply uncertainty. WTI crude fell $0.41 from Thursday's close to $76.88 per barrel as of Friday morning (per the live tape), recovering from a session low near $75.82. The geopolitical premium is back in oil: the August 5 US-Iran-Oman 60-day deal framework is intact in concept, but the restrictive conditions in the August 6 draft plan have extended the uncertainty window. The 5.1% WTD gain in WTI (last Friday's close near $73.11 per the prior week's data) represents the cumulative geopolitical risk premium. XLE's Thursday close of $58.16 (+1.48%) reflects the oil-market re-pricing.

**10-Year Yield Climbs to 4.67% — 5 Basis Points Above Thursday's Close as Bond Market Pre-Positions for NFP.** The 10-year Treasury yield has risen 5 basis points from Thursday's 4.62% close to 4.67% (per yfinance Friday morning), a modest but notable overnight move ahead of the 8:30 AM payrolls release. The direction is clear: the bond market is not pricing a weak jobs report — it is pricing resilience or strength. TLT has correspondingly eased to $82.52 from Thursday's $83.00 close (-0.58%), as the fixed-income market adjusts duration exposure ahead of a potentially hot print. The 4.67% level is meaningful: the 4.80% area remains the next technical resistance, and a payrolls print above +150,000 would likely test that level and pressure equity multiples. The 4.60–4.65% zone that SPX successfully navigated through the first week of August is now being re-tested from above.

## What to Watch at the Open

- **NFP print vs consensus +70K — and the 10-year yield reaction at 8:30–9:00 AM ET.** A print in the +90K to +110K range (in-line with the soft-landing labor trend) would likely keep ES flat-to-slightly-higher and prevent the 10Y from testing 4.80%. A print below +75K re-opens the "labor market deceleration" concern and would likely pressure equities 0.5–1.0% while pushing the 10Y back below 4.60%. A print above +150K would validate the Warsh Fed hawkish-dissent posture (3 dissents at the July FOMC) and likely push the 10Y through 4.75% with corresponding equity multiple compression.
- **Energy sector follow-through — XLE held Thursday's +1.45% gain despite broader market weakness.** With WTI sitting at $76.88 (5.1% WTD from last Friday's ~$73.11 close) and the Iran Hormuz draft plan uncertainty extending the geopolitical premium, watch whether XLE can hold the bid above $58.00 or whether the oil-linked strength fades as the broader tape takes direction from NFP. The $75–80 WTI equilibrium range remains the base case absent a full Hormuz reopening or escalation.
- **Megacap tech and Nasdaq relative strength in pre-market (NQ +0.44% vs ES +0.16%).** The +4.10% XLK week-to-date surge through Thursday is being digested in two modest down days (-0.31% Wednesday, -0.31% Thursday), and the pre-market Nasdaq outperformance suggests the digestion may be largely complete ahead of the NFP catalyst. Watch QQQ and XLK at the open: if they hold the pre-market bid after the NFP print, the AI capex durability thesis remains intact and the structural bull case (S&P 500 in the 7,650–7,800 range with a path higher) holds.

## The Structural Read

The tape is in a coherent pre-NFP posture: modest risk-on in global markets (Hang Seng +0.54%, DAX +0.83%, FTSE +0.70%), energy leading on Iran geopolitical risk, yields elevated but not in runaway compression, and Nasdaq futures outperforming S&P futures in the pre-market. This is orderly consolidation — not the panicked de-risking that precedes a correction. The S&P 500 is 2.94% above last Friday's close (per yfinance's 5-day data), meaning the week has been net positive even after the Tuesday +1.79% surge and the two-day digestion. The structural thesis — AI capex durability across mega-cap tech (five Q2 bars validated: TSMC, MSFT, AMZN, Palantir, AMD; NVDA expected late August), US manufacturing reshoring, disinflation trend holding, and a Fed that is data-dependent rather than pre-committed to cuts — has not changed. It is being tested, not broken.

The 10-year yield at 4.67% is the most important internal market signal heading into the print. Yields 5 basis points higher than Thursday's close means the bond market is not pricing a soft or weak NFP — it is pricing a number consistent with continued labor market resilience. If the print is in-line (+90K to +110K), the yield stabilization near 4.67% and the equity market's ability to absorb a +20-30bp yield move without breaking the 7,650–7,800 range would be a significant bullish technical confirmation. If the print is hot (+150K+), the 4.80% test on the 10-year becomes the next narrative — and the equity market's reaction to that yield level (multiple compression vs. earnings-can-dominate framing) will define the next five weeks of tape. The VIX at 15.26 — below its 1-year mean of ~18 by roughly 12% — reflects option-market complacency consistent with range-bound positioning. A hot NFP print pushing the VIX toward 18 would be the signal that the next move in rates, not the next move in earnings, is the dominant market force.

**Bottom line:** Friday is NFP day — the July Employment Summary at 8:30 AM ET is the single most important data release before the August 20–21 FOMC. The tape is positioned for a resilient or in-line print (yields higher, Nasdaq outperforming), and the week is net positive for equities (SPX +2.94% WTD). A print in the +90K to +130K range consolidates the soft-landing thesis and keeps the S&P 500 in its 7,650–7,800 range with a path to 7,800+ by month-end. What would change the read: a sub-75K payrolls number sending the 10-year below 4.55% and SPX below 7,600 (recession fear re-emergence), or a +150K+ print pushing the 10-year toward 4.80% and testing whether the equity multiple at 27–28x earnings can absorb higher-for-longer rates on the AI capex thesis alone.
